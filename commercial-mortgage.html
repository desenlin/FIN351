1 · First mortgage

Has the senior claim on property collateral and normally carries the lowest cost in the debt stack.

2 · Mezzanine debt

Is generally secured by the ownership interest, adds leverage, and carries a higher rate than senior debt.

3 · Refinancing cost

Origination charges and prepayment penalties are paid now in exchange for possible future debt-service savings.

4 · Decision rule

Refinance when incremental NPV is positive—or equivalently, when the refinancing IRR exceeds the opportunity cost.

Mortgage replacement

2. Should the borrower refinance?

The new loan pays off the old loan's remaining balance. The incremental cash flows are the monthly debt-service savings, less origination fees and the old loan's prepayment penalty. If the analysis ends early, the difference between the two remaining balances is also recognized.

NPV = PV(payment savings + terminal balance benefit) − refinancing costRefinance if NPV > 0; equivalently, if refinancing IRR > the discount rate.

Interpretation

Key takeaways

Leverage magnifies

Mezz debt reduces required equity. It raises equity return when its marginal cost is below the return generated before adding mezz debt—and lowers return when the inequality reverses.

Seniority matters

The first mortgage retains the senior property claim. Mezzanine debt is generally secured by the borrower's ownership interest and therefore commands a higher rate.

Rate reduction is insufficient

A lower new rate does not automatically justify refinancing. Payment savings must exceed fees, penalties, balance effects, and the opportunity cost of capital.
